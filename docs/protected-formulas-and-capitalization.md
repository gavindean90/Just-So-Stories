# Protected Formulas and Capitalization

This sheet governs the narrow editorial pass on the six canonical story texts.
It distinguishes collection architecture from mechanical inconsistency so that
copy edits do not flatten purposeful oral variation.

## Collection-wide formulas

Preserve these forms and their story-specific completions:

- `Listen closely and listen well, O Little Night-Wanderer.`
- `For this happened back in the Very-Near-Now.`
- `Right on the ... edge of the Sprawling Suburbs.`
- `Now, you must understand this, O Little Night-Wanderer.`
- `And because it was magic, it stuck to him/her fast.`
- `And that is the reason, to this very minute, that ...`
- `Just to make strictly sure ...`
- `So, the very next time ...`

Purposeful variants are also protected:

- Coyote's Map-running Legs are magic and `carried him fast`.
- Opossum's Crown of Babies is `bound by love`, not magic.
- Widow's integrated transformation is woven and washed over her; it does not
  use the stuck-fast formula.
- Seagull's double-deficit opening remains irregular pending oral proof.
- Raccoon's brief etiological curtain does not receive an added encounter or
  bedtime coda.

Capitalized acquisition names may return in deliberately shortened forms. A
shortened inventory or coda name is not automatically a consistency error.

## Protected character and acquisition epithets

### Coyote

- Before-state: `lean and loping Wordless Coyote`
- After coat acquisition: `lean and loping Ghost-grey Coyote`
- `Tawny, Dust-grey Ghost Coat`
- `Long, Loping, Map-running Legs`
- `Moon-calling Voice`
- Crow's claim that Coyote is the city's `master` expresses Crow's worldview
  and must not be harmonized with Coyote's belonging-centered ending.

### Raccoon

- `round and rolling Clever Mother Raccoon`
- `clumsy, mud-mitten, bumble-thick Thumping Paws`
- `Night-black Mask of the Bandit`
- `Ringed Tail of the Phantom`
- `tiny, tickle-touch, lock-picking Thieving Thumbs`
- The click-a-clack, snick-a-snack and tap-a-tap Crows are three distinct
  individuals. Their different beak sounds are intentional.

### Skunk

- `proud and pompous Aristocrat Skunk`
- `Blinding, Bone-white Stripe`
- `High-held, Warning Plume Tail`
- `Foul and Fuming, Sour-smelling Cloud`
- `Heavy, Hard Warning Stomp`
- The repeated `A king does not ...` structure is inviolable.

### Seagull

- Before-state: `shy and silent Shoreline Bird`
- After-state: `bold and brazen Seagull Thief`
- `Cloud-white, Sun-blinding Cloak`
- `Sharp and Yellow Snatching-Hook`
- `Laughing, Yowling, Sky-Thief Cry`
- `Great and Greedy Sky-Dive`

### Opossum

- `slow and silent Mother Opossum`
- `Clinging, Crawling Crown of Babies`
- `Snaking, Snatching Pink-flesh Tail`
- `Ghost-white, Staring Skeleton Mask`
- `Great and Ghastly Fainting Fall`

### Widow

- Before-state: `small and silent Dust-brown Spider`
- After-state: `patient and poisonous Red-marked Widow`
- `Night-black Coat`
- `Red-marked Warning`

## Protected sound vocabulary

Preserve spelling, order and punctuation where the text uses these as audible
identity or action cues:

- Owl: `Whoo-whoo-whoo.`
- Hound: `Clink-clank-clink.`, `Sniff-snuff-snarl!`,
  `Snap-clack-chomp!`, `Sniff-sniff-snort.`
- Opossum's Cat: `Swish-a-sway!`
- Widow: `Tremble-tick.`
- Seagull's surf: `Crash... shhh... sigh.`
- Skunk: `THUMP-THUMP-THUMP!`
- Seagull: `SQUAWK-SCREECH-YOWL!!!` followed by `SNATCH.`

## Mechanical house style for this pass

- Use straight double quotation marks for dialogue, matching the majority of
  the canonical texts.
- Use three periods for performed trailing sounds and pauses; do not substitute
  a single ellipsis glyph.
- Use `grey` rather than `gray`.
- Preserve exclamation-point counts in sound effects.
- Preserve deliberate sentence fragments, repeated conjunctions, repeated
  epithets and capitalization that marks spoken names.
- Normalize a full acquisition epithet only when it is clearly intended to be
  repeated in full; do not expand deliberately shortened references.
