# Read-Aloud Prosody and Connective-Tissue Audit

Baseline: `main` at `0e684f6`

Scope: all six canonical story texts, read in collection order. This audit records
only YELLOW and RED passages. Passages not listed were classified GREEN and
left alone, including deliberately plain landings after denser language.

## How the Coyote Found His Moon-Calling Voice

No YELLOW or RED passages. The apparent candidates in the transitions after
the first and second gifts already use purposeful repetition, sentence-length
contrast, internal echoes, and a long, loping syntax suited to Coyote. Further
decoration would narrow the useful quiet around the set pieces.

## How the Raccoon Got Her Thieving Thumbs

### RED — Exposure beneath the porch light

Starts: “The yard was suddenly flooded with a thick and Honey-yellow light.”

Four consecutive subject-verb declarations report the exposure, then two more
report the escape. The information is clear, but the stresses do not carry
Raccoon's quick, scrambling panic. The passage needs one clipped landing and
one directional run of phrases, without adding a confrontation.

### YELLOW — Crossing the yard after the light goes out

Starts: “She was hidden. She was a phantom.”

The two short declarations land well. The following compound sentence resumes
plot delivery without quite carrying their pulse toward the bins. One rhythmic
bridge can turn stillness back into motion.

### YELLOW — The third Crow revealed with the prize

Starts: “He was calm, and he was eating a piece of the prize!”

The repeated “he was” construction is informational rather than performative.
A small oral inversion can make the reveal tap more lightly while preserving
the exclamation and the sticky-sweet prize.

### RED — Journey to the Moon-bright Puddle

Starts: “So she waddled off to the edge of the street.”

Four short declarative units explain where she went, what she found, and what
she did. The standalone acquisition name and paw refrain are strong, but the
sentences between them do not make the motion of the mouth match the motion of
the hand. A light inversion and cleaner linkage can restore the tactile rhythm.

### YELLOW — Return to the latch

Starts: “She crept back across the yard.”

Three consecutive declarations efficiently place Raccoon, the sleeping
Two-Legs, and the latch. A parallel location phrase can give the approach a
quiet tiptoe before the already-effective held breath and “Click.”

## How the Skunk Demanded Respect

No YELLOW or RED passages. The connective tissue is already governed by the
measured alternation of warning, refusal, acquisition, and return. Its repeated
short declarations sound like deliberate steps in Skunk's march, not flat
exposition.

## How the Seagull Snatched the Golden Fry

### YELLOW — Recovery from the missed second dive

Starts: “He grabbed nothing but the empty, salty air.”

The first sentence has good mouthfeel, and the closing inventory clearly
explains the failure. Between them, the static phrase “He was completely
defeated” briefly stalls a story whose native motion is aerial. One concrete
upward beat can carry him from the miss into the Pelican's appearance.

## How the Opossum Cheated the Hound

### YELLOW — Flight to the fence

Starts: “She ran for the edge of the yard.”

The three declarations create urgency, but “ran” does not call back Opossum's
established shuffling physicality. One paired verb can make the pursuit more
character-specific without slowing it.

### YELLOW — Rising after the Hound leaves

Starts: “She waited until the yard was perfectly quiet.”

The sequence is clear but evenly reported: waited, twitched, opened, picked up,
carried. A slight syntactic turn and a short upward beat can let the release of
tension scramble again.

## How the Black Widow Wove the Red Warning

### YELLOW — Repair after the Broom's attack

Starts: “She gathered up her fallen egg-sac...”

Three important recovery actions are tucked into one even sentence. Giving
each action its own beat better matches Widow's ticking, incantatory rhythm and
makes the following Lizard entrance feel earned, without enlarging the moment.

## Revision record

The audit produced nine interventions in four stories. Coyote and Skunk were
left unchanged. The excerpts below contain every meaningful prose change.

### Raccoon

| Passage | Before | After | Prosodic reason |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Porch-light exposure | “The yard was suddenly flooded... She was completely caught... flashed in the sudden light.” | “The yard flooded all at once... Completely caught... flashed.” | Removes passive drag and duplicate light language; the fragment gives the panic a hard landing. |
| Escape | “She turned and ran. She scrambled all the way back...” | “She turned and ran. Across the grass, past the porch, all the way back...” | A three-part directional run lets the reader accelerate with her while preserving the approved action: she only runs away. |
| Crossing to the bins | “She crossed the rest of the yard and finally reached her prize.” | “So across the rest of the yard she crept, until at last she reached her prize.” | The inversion and paired time markers bridge the stillness of “She was a phantom” back into furtive movement. |
| Third Crow reveal | “He was calm, and he was eating a piece of the prize!” | “There he sat, calm as could be, eating a piece of the prize!” | Replaces repeated copulas with a light oral reveal and keeps the tap-a-tap Crow's entrance playful. |
| Moon-bright Puddle | “She found a pool... She reached into the water.” | “There she found a pool... She reached down into the water.” | A place marker and directional verb break the bare subject-verb chain and guide the voice from place to hand. |
| Return to the latch | “The Tall Two-Legs were asleep. She went to the plastic and she tickled the latch.” | “Behind the squarish windows, the Tall Two-Legs slept. At the plastic lid, she tickled the latch.” | Parallel location phrases create a quiet, tiptoeing approach to the held breath and “Click.” |

### Seagull

| Passage | Before | After | Prosodic reason |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Missed second dive | “He was completely defeated.” | “Back toward the glare he flapped, completely defeated.” | Keeps the failure in motion and supplies one upward beat before the Pelican looks down. |

### Opossum

| Passage | Before | After | Prosodic reason |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Flight to the fence | “She ran for the edge of the yard.” | “She shuffled and scrambled for the edge of the yard.” | Restores her established physical rhythm and adds pursuit energy through stressed consonance. |
| Recovery | “She waited until... Then she twitched... She opened... She picked herself up...” | “She waited. Until... she waited. Then her... nose twitched. Her eyes opened. Up she scrambled...” | An anxious repeat holds the pause; short body-part beats and the upward inversion release it. |

### Black Widow

| Passage | Before | After | Prosodic reason |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Repair after the Broom | “She gathered... patched... and hid...” | “She gathered... She patched... She hid...” | Three equal beats make recovery sound deliberate and incantatory before the helper appears. |

## Protected material

The pass intentionally made no changes to the collection openings and endings,
established character or acquisition epithets, named magical traits, sound
effects, acquisition and etiological formulas, transformations, climaxes, or
the major repeated structures. Deliberately plain contrast sentences were also
retained wherever they worked aloud.
