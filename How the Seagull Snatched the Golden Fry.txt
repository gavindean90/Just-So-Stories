Listen closely and listen well, O Little Night-Wanderer. For this happened back in the Very-Near-Now. Right on the sandy, sun-baked edge of the Sprawling Suburbs.
In those days, he had no Laughing Cry at all.
No Yellow Snatching-Hook.
The shy and silent Shoreline Bird.
He had a bold, bright-eyed mind, but a polite and patient, waiting-in-the-sand sort of heart. He wished to taste the treasures of the Tall Two-Legs. But he would not steal. He would not snatch. He would not swoop. He only possessed his waiting.
His polite, patient, pacing-in-the-sand.
Now, you must understand this, O Little Night-Wanderer. The Tall Two-Legs loved the sandy, sun-baked edge. And every summer afternoon, they carried out the paper cups.
The Red and White Salty-Smelling Cups.
They carried them down the boardwalk with a crackle-crunch-crinkle. And they held them high in the bright, blinding sun.
Inside of those cups waited the absolute best of the feast.
The Golden-fried, Salt-kissed Potato Sticks.
One afternoon, he hopped up to the wooden boardwalk.
The shy and silent Shoreline Bird.
He smelled the salt and the sizzle on the wandering wind. A Golden-fried, Salt-kissed Potato Stick. He thought of his empty belly. He wanted to ask for a bite.
But before he could take three polite steps, a fat shadow waddled past.
The Plump and Pecking Boardwalk Pigeon.
Coo-coo-croon.
"You wait and you walk," the Pigeon bubbled and bobbed. "But walkers only get the crumbs in the cracks. The best bites are held high in the hands. You must strike from the sky. Hide your shadow in the sun."
So he flew up into the bright, blazing sky. He rolled in the puffy, floating clouds. He painted his belly with the bright white glare, and he painted his back with the storm-grey mist. He gathered the colors of the blinding sky.
His Cloud-white, Sun-blinding Cloak.
And because it was magic, it stuck to him fast.
He was hidden in the glare. He circled and he soared. He saw a Tall Two-Legs holding a Golden-fried, Salt-kissed Potato Stick. He fluttered down and he grabbed for the prize.
Slip-slide-fumble!
His beak was too flat and too smooth. The potato stick slipped right out of his mouth and fell onto the wooden boards. The Plump and Pecking Boardwalk Pigeon ate it in one bite.
He had the cloak, but he lacked the catch. He had failed.
Down in the wet and winding tide pools, a hard shadow looked up.
The Pinching, Prowling Sand Crab.
Snip-snap-snick!
"You fumble and you fall," the Crab bubbled and hissed. "A flat mouth cannot steal a fry. You must pinch. You must grab. You must forge a hook."
So he flew to the very edge of the water. He dipped his beak into the bright yellow reflection of the sun. He pinched the golden light, and he bent the very tip of his beak into a sharp, downward point. He forged a brand new tool.
His Sharp and Yellow Snatching-Hook.
And because it was magic, it stuck to him fast.
He flew back up into the blinding glare. He had the cloak, and he had the hook. He circled and he soared. He dove for a Tall Two-Legs holding a Golden-fried, Salt-kissed Potato Stick.
But the Tall Two-Legs saw him coming! The Two-Legs pulled the hand away.
Whoosh-miss-flap!
He grabbed nothing but the empty, salty air. He was completely defeated. He had the cloak, and he had the hook, but the Tall Two-Legs were too fast.
Out past the breaking, bubbling surf, a heavy shadow looked down.
The Great and Gliding Pelican.
Splash-slap-swallow!
"You sneak and you silence," the Pelican croaked and coughed. "But silence gives them time to move. You must startle the hand. You must scream like the storm."
So he opened his polite and patient beak. He swallowed the screech of the wind. He swallowed the holler of the hurricane. He swallowed the yowling, laughing, squawking sound of the wild, unruly sea. He possessed a brand new voice.
His Laughing, Yowling, Sky-Thief Cry.
And because it was magic, it stuck to him fast.
He was ready.
He flew high into the blinding sun.
With his Cloud-white Cloak.
He locked his eyes on the prize.
With his Yellow Snatching-Hook.
He opened his throat.
And he performed the Great and Greedy Sky-Dive.
He dropped from the clouds. He opened his beak.
SQUAWK-SCREECH-YOWL!!!
The Tall Two-Legs jumped! The Tall Two-Legs shivered and shouted! The Tall Two-Legs dropped the prize!
SNATCH.
He hooked the prize.
That Golden-fried, Salt-kissed Potato Stick.
He swallowed it whole, and he vanished like a cloud back into the sky, the boldest thief on the boardwalk.
And that is the reason, to this very minute, that he never waits patiently in the sand.
The bold and brazen Seagull Thief.
It shows he belongs to the blinding sky. And you will notice he watches the Red and White Cups from the clouds. And he never asks politely. Just to make strictly sure the Tall Two-Legs drop their guard.
To startle the hand.
To hook the prize.
With that Laughing, Yowling, Sky-Thief Cry.
So, the very next time you lie in your bed in the dark, O Little Night-Wanderer.
Pull the heavy blankets right up to your chin.
Close your heavy, sleepy eyes.
Listen past the streetlamps.
Listen past the roaring roads.
Listen for the sound of the sandy edge.
Where the ocean waves are rolling in.
Rolling over the sand.
Rolling under the moon.
Crash... shhh... sigh.
Washing the boardwalk clean.
Crash... shhh... sigh.
Rocking the shoreline to sleep.
Crash... shhh... sigh.
Fast asleep.
