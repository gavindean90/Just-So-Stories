Listen closely and listen well, O Little Night-Wanderer. For this happened back in the Very-Near-Now. Right on the rough and ragged edge of the Sprawling Suburbs.
In those days, he had no Moon-calling Voice at all. No Moon-calling Voice. The lean and loping Ghost-grey Coyote.
He had a restless, ranging mind, and long, lazy legs that covered the miles. He wished to walk where he wished. He wished to go where he went. But nobody knew he was there. And nobody knew he belonged. He only possessed his wandering. His wide, wordless, unknowable wandering.
Now, you must understand this, O Little Night-Wanderer. The Suburbs were a broken-up, patch-worked thing. Divided by fences and parking lots and the long, rolling rivers of the roads. There was no single wood. No one single wild. Only scattered scraps of shadow, stitched together by the dark.
One midnight, he stepped out from the edge of the trees. The lean and loping Ghost-grey Coyote. He smelled the far and fractured patches of the wild on the wandering wind. They were everywhere, and they were nowhere. Too spread, too scattered, too severed to find.
He lifted his long nose to the night sky.
High in the hollow of a dead oak tree, a round shadow blinked down. The Old Brown Owl with the whoo-whoo-whoo eyes.
Whoo-whoo-whoo.
"You search like a stranger," she whispered and sighed. "You walk like a ghost, but you shine in the dark. You move through this wild, but you do not yet wear it. You must borrow the grey of the neither-here-nor-there."
So he waded into the tall, dry, dust-grey grasses at the edge of the road. He rolled in the pale, chalky grit of the gravel. He pressed himself flat against the color of the cool concrete and the dead winter lawns. He gathered the grey of the neither-here-nor-there. His Tawny, Dust-grey Ghost Coat. And because it was magic, it stuck to him fast.
He walked out into the Sprawling Suburbs. With his wide, wordless, unknowable wandering. The Tall Two-Legs looked out their squarish windows. They saw the drifting shadows and the shifting scraps of dark. But they did not see him.
But the Suburbs were still broken and scattered. The scraps of shadow were still far apart. He could move through the in-between, but he could not find the edges of his own world.
The Old Brown Owl blinked down from a bent, bending streetlamp.
Whoo-whoo-whoo.
"You wander like water," she whispered and sighed. "Water finds every crack, every culvert, every low and hidden place. You must learn the whole map. Stretch your long legs until you know every edge."
So he ran. He loped and he leaped, he trotted and tracked. He ran the highway medians and the deep, dark ditches. He ran the railroad edges and the silent, stone-filled cemeteries. He mapped the margins. He learned the lines. He built the broken wild into a single, secret country only he could read. With his Long, Loping, Map-running Legs. And because it was magic, they carried him fast.
He knew every scrap of shadow now. He wore the color of the in-between. He moved through the whole fractured wild like a needle through cloth. With his wide, wordless, unknowable wandering.
But he was still silent. And the Suburbs were loud.
They rumbled and roared, they screeched and they sighed. The Roaring Metal Monsters. The wailing, warning Tall Two-Legs Alarm Sirens. The noise swallowed everything, including him. He was invisible. He was everywhere. But he was unknown.
Then, on the cold wire of a telephone line, a sleek shadow dropped down. The City Crow with the soft, sweeping wings.
Swoosh-swish-sway.
"You are a ghost," he whispered and hummed. "And ghosts are forgotten. You wear the coat and you run the map. But this place does not know you are its master. You must answer it back. You must soothe it to sleep."
Just then, far across the fractured dark, a Tall Two-Legs Alarm Siren sighed into the sky.
Waaaaaail...
A long, lonely wail in the dark.
The lean and loping Ghost-grey Coyote lifted his long grey nose. He opened his throat. He gathered every scrap of the wild he had mapped, every mile of the margin, every ditch and median and moonlit cemetery. He pulled it all up from his chest.
And he answered.
Awooooooo...
A long, low, lingering sound. It rolled off the brick walls. It drifted down the cul-de-sacs. It climbed the fences and crossed the parking lots and leaped the roaring roads. It stitched every scattered scrap of shadow into a single, singing, unmistakable thing.
His Moon-calling Voice. And because it was magic, it stuck to him fast.
The Tall Two-Legs stopped. They stood in their driveways and tilted their heads. They listened to the long, low, lingering sound. They could not see him. They could not find him. But now, every last one of them knew.
He was there. He had always been there. And this was his city too.
The lean and loping Ghost-grey Coyote turned around. He trotted back into the in-between, wearing the color of the neither-here-nor-there. With his wide, wordless, unknowable wandering.
And that is the reason, to this very minute, that he always wears the grey of the cool concrete and the dead winter grass. The lean and loping Ghost-grey Coyote. It shows he belongs to the in-between. And you will notice he knows every margin and median and moonlit edge. And every time the Tall Two-Legs fill the night with their lonely noise, he opens his long grey throat. Just to make strictly sure they remember. That this patch-worked wild is his country. And he will sing it to sleep. With his long, low, Moon-calling Voice.
So, the very next time you lie in your bed in the dark, O Little Night-Wanderer. Pull the heavy blankets right up to your chin. Close your heavy, sleepy eyes.
Listen past the streetlamps. Listen past the roaring roads. And if you are very, very still...
You will hear him singing the shadows to sleep. Somewhere out past the last fence. Somewhere in the long grey margins. Somewhere in the neither-here-nor-there.
The lean and loping Ghost-grey Coyote. Stitching the wild back together. Singing you to sleep. With his long, low, Moon-calling Voice.
