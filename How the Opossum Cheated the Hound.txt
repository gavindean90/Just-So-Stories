Listen closely and listen well, O Little Night-Wanderer. For this happened back in the Very-Near-Now. Right on the rough and ragged edge of the Sprawling Suburbs.
In those days, she had no Pink-flesh Tail at all. No Pink-flesh Tail. The slow and silent Mother Opossum.
She had a fierce and fearless heart, but short, stubby, and shuffling legs. She wished to keep her babies completely safe. But she could not sprint. She could not spring. She could not soar. She only possessed her shuffling steps. Her slow, swaying, side-to-side waddle.
Now, you must understand this, O Little Night-Wanderer. The Tall Two-Legs kept monsters in their manicured yards. Fast, fierce, and furious things. And the fiercest of all was the watcher of the grass. The Heavy, Howling Silver-chain Hound.
He marched across the grass with a clink-clank-clink. He searched the shadows with a cold, wet nose. The Heavy, Howling Silver-chain Hound.
One midnight, she stepped out from the edge of the woods. The slow and silent Mother Opossum. She heard the heavy breathing on the wandering wind. A fast, fierce, and furious monster. She thought of her helpless babies. She wanted to slip across the yard and hide them in the silent shadows.
But before she could take three tiny steps, the Hound dug straight into the dirt.
Scratch-scatter-scrape!
He found the hidden hole! She was completely caught.
She gathered and she grabbed. She scooped and she settled. She wove her wonderful babies right into her thick, grey fur. She built a living basket on her back. The Clinging, Crawling Crown of Babies. And because it was bound by love, it stuck to her fast.
She ran for the edge of the yard. The slow and silent Mother Opossum. She reached the tall wooden fence. The Hound was right behind her!
Sniff-snuff-snarl!
She jumped for the wood. She tried to climb the flat, smooth boards. But her fluffy, feathery tail slipped right off the grain.
Scratch-scuff-slip!
She fell backward into the short, sharp grass. She was too heavy with her Clinging Crown, and her fur was far too soft.
High up on the wooden fence, a shadow looked down. The Stray Black Cat with the swish-a-sway tail.
Swish-a-sway! "You slip like silk," he hissed and he hummed. "Fur is for the freezing, but flesh is for the grabbing. Shed the fluff. Wrap the wood."
So she stripped the fluff completely from her tail. She traded her soft fur for a gripping, grabbing tool. She wrapped the bare pink flesh tightly around the wooden beam. Her Snaking, Snatching Pink-flesh Tail. And because it was magic, it stuck to her fast.
She gripped and she grabbed, she climbed and she crawled. She hauled herself high onto the top of the fence line.
But the Hound did not give up. He leaped high into the air. He snapped his heavy, hunting jaws right at her toes.
Snap-clack-chomp!
She was on the fence, but the Hound was not afraid. He was waiting for her to fall.
Low on the patio porch, the shadow looked up. The Stray Black Cat with the swish-a-sway tail.
Swish-a-sway! "You shake like a leaf," he hissed and he hummed. "You look exactly like a meal. You must look like a memory. Paint your face with the dust of the bones."
So she rubbed her dark and dusty face right into the pale, chalky moonlight. She painted deep black rings around her eyes. She made herself look like a staring, startling skull. Her Ghost-white, Staring Skeleton Mask. And because it was magic, it stuck to her fast.
She glared down at the Hound. With her Ghost-white Mask, and her Pink-flesh Tail, and her Clinging Crown of Babies.
The Hound stepped back. He barked and he battered, he bounded and he bashed his heavy weight against the wooden fence!
CRACK!!!
The wood shook! Her tail slipped! She fell!
THUD.
She landed in the short, sharp grass. The slow and silent Mother Opossum.
She was completely defeated. The Hound stood directly over her. She had her crown, and she had her tail, and she had her mask, but she was trapped on the ground. She had failed.
Then, deep in the garden grass, the shadow whispered out. The Stray Black Cat with the swish-a-sway tail.
Swish-a-sway! "You breathe and you beat," he hissed and he hummed. The Stray Black Cat with the swish-a-sway tail.
"The living are for biting. But the dead are left alone. You must trick the teeth. Become the dust."
The Hound opened his terrible, dripping jaws.
So she dropped her jaw. She hushed her breath. She slowed her beating heart. She performed the Great and Ghastly Fainting Fall.
She lay perfectly, completely, absolutely still.
The Hound leaned down. He sniffed her ghost-white face. He sniffed her pink-flesh tail.
Sniff-sniff-snort.
The Hound could not hear her breathe. The Hound could not hear her heart beat. She did not shake.
The Hound was bored. A hound does not hunt the dust. He turned his heavy head. He marched away across the manicured lawn.
Clink-clank-clink.
She waited until the yard was perfectly quiet. Then she twitched her ghost-white nose. She opened her eyes. She picked herself up, and she carried her babies safely into the woods.
And that is the reason, to this very minute, that she carries her children on her back. The slow and silent Mother Opossum. It shows she will never leave them behind. And you will notice she wraps her naked tail around the branches. And she wears the skull of a ghost. Just to make strictly sure that if the monsters ever catch her... She is ready to trick the teeth. Ready to drop her jaw. And perform the Great and Ghastly Fainting Fall.
So, the very next time you walk the wooden fence line in the dark. Shine your flashlight up into the leaves, O Little Night-Wanderer. Watch the vines. Watch the shifting branches. And if you look closely in the light... You might just catch the gleam of a Ghost-white Mask. You might just see the snatch of a Pink-flesh Tail. Up above the Heavy, Howling Silver-chain Hounds. Keeping the Clinging, Crawling Crown of Babies completely safe.
