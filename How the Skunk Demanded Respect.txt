Listen closely and listen well, O Little Night-Wanderer. For this happened back in the Very-Near-Now. Right on the rough and ragged edge of the Sprawling Suburbs.
In those days, he had no Bone-white Stripe at all. No Bone-white Stripe. The proud and pompous Aristocrat Skunk.
He had a stubborn, swelling pride, and a slow, strolling stride. He wished to walk down the center of the street. But he would not scramble. He would not scurry. He would not sprint. He only possessed his pride. His slow, steady, arrogant strut.
Now, you must understand this, O Little Night-Wanderer. The Suburbs were made for the fast and the furious. They belonged to the Roaring Metal Monsters, and the fierce, furry watcher of the grass. The Heavy, Howling Silver-chain Hound.
One midnight, he stepped out from the edge of the woods. The proud and pompous Aristocrat Skunk. He smelled the sweet, sticky dew on the wandering wind. He wanted to take his evening stroll down the Silver-cold Concrete.
Deep in the shadows of the Great Green Grumbling Plastic Bins, a masked face peeked out. The round and rolling Clever Mother Raccoon.
"You march like a fool!" she hissed and she hummed. "You are slow! You are soft! You must scamper and hide! The Hound is hunting in the dark!"
"A king does not scamper," he sniffed and he sneered. The proud and pompous Aristocrat Skunk. "A king does not hide. I belong in the light. I will make myself known."
So he walked into the glow of the Pale and Peeping Midnight Moon. He bathed his back in the chalky, glowing light. He painted a bold, bright path from his nose to his toes. His Blinding, Bone-white Stripe. And because it was magic, it stuck to him fast.
He walked out into the yard. With his slow, steady, arrogant strut.
But the summer weeds were thick and tall. The grass swallowed the glowing light.
The Hound came bounding across the lawn!
Sniff-snuff-snarl!
The Hound could not see the stripe in the grass. He almost trampled the king!
"You are hidden by the weeds!" the Clever Mother Raccoon whispered from the bins. "You must run for the woods!"
"A king does not run," he sniffed and he sneered. The proud and pompous Aristocrat Skunk. "If the grass hides the paint, I must raise up a flag."
So he gathered the wind. He puffed and he fluffed. He blew the night breeze straight into his flat, black tail until it stood up tall and thick like a tower. His High-held, Warning Plume Tail. And because it was magic, it stuck to him fast.
He walked back into the yard. With his Bone-white Stripe, and his High-held Tail, and his slow, steady, arrogant strut.
The Hound saw the flag. But a flag has no teeth. A flag has no claws. The Hound did not care. He lowered his heavy head. He charged.
Snap-clack-chomp!
"The beast does not care!" the Clever Mother Raccoon whispered from the bins. "You must fight or you must flee!"
"A king does not bite," he sniffed and he sneered. The proud and pompous Aristocrat Skunk. "Biting is for beasts. I will teach him respect."
So he waddled to the edge of the garden. He climbed right into the wet and rotting compost heap. He gathered the sour, stinging, suffocating smell of the rotting roots. He swallowed it deep into his belly. The Foul and Fuming, Sour-smelling Cloud. And because it was magic, it stuck to him fast.
He had his Stripe, and he had his Tail, and he had his Cloud. He stepped back onto the Silver-cold Concrete.
The Hound bounded forward.
Clink-clank-clink.
The Skunk did not scramble. He did not scurry. He did not flee. He stood perfectly, absolutely still.
He raised his High-held Plume Tail. He flashed his Blinding Bone-white Stripe. But a king does not draw his sword without giving a warning.
So he lifted his front paws. He struck the Silver-cold Concrete.
THUMP-THUMP-THUMP!
He performed the Heavy, Hard Warning Stomp.
The Hound stopped.
Sniff-sniff-snort.
The Hound smelled the sour, stinging magic. The Hound saw the glowing white flag. The Hound heard the heavy, hard drumbeat of the king.
The Hound whined. He tucked his feathery tail. He backed slowly away into the grass.
The proud and pompous Aristocrat Skunk turned around. And he took his evening stroll. With his slow, steady, arrogant strut.
And that is the reason, to this very minute, that he never scurries and he never runs. The proud and pompous Aristocrat Skunk. It shows he demands your absolute respect. And you will notice he paints his back with the moon. And he raises his flag high in the grass. Just to make strictly sure that you see him coming. To stay bold. To stay brave. And stay proud. With his slow, steady, arrogant strut.
So, the very next time you walk down the cracked concrete sidewalk. Look into the tall summer grass, O Little Night-Wanderer. Watch the shadows. Watch the shifting weeds. And if you look closely in the dark... You might just catch the gleam of a Bone-white Stripe. You might just see the wave of a High-held Plume Tail. And if you know what is good for you... You will stand perfectly still. You will hold your breath. And let him pass with his slow, steady, arrogant strut.
